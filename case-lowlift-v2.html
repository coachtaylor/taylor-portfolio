LowLift Fitness Case study
01 Case study · Health & wellness · Live

Built for the first ten reps, not the next PR.

LowLift is a fitness app for the people the rest of the market actively scares off — the absolute beginner, the post‑injury return, the gym‑shy. Designed, built, and shipped solo over four months.

// 01 — The problem

The fitness market sells to the already-fit.

Open any top‑grossing fitness app. Day 1 is a 45‑minute HIIT. Day 2 is a heavier 45‑minute HIIT. There is no Day 0.

// TP, opening notes · MAR 2024
73%
of new fitness app users quit within 90 days
// PUBLIC APP-RETENTION BENCHMARKS. THE COHORT I CARED ABOUT QUIT FASTER.
A

The “easy” tier isn’t easy.

I audited 12 top apps. Their “beginner” plans assumed a 30‑minute baseline. Anyone deconditioned bounces in week one — and blames themselves, not the program.

B

Shame is the real churn driver.

In 40+ user interviews, the word that kept coming back wasn’t “hard.” It was “embarrassing.” Apps that show jacked silhouettes lose this user before login.

C

The gap is huge — and ignored.

~60% of US adults don’t meet activity guidelines. The market chases the 15% who already lift. Nobody fights for the bottom of the funnel because it’s “low LTV.” Wrong.

// 02 — Process

Four months. No agency. No committee.

SOLO_LOOP

One person doing discovery, design, code, and shipping. The loop tightens every week — interview Monday, build Tue–Thu, test Friday, repeat.

01
// WK 1–3 · DISCOVERY

Talk to the people apps had already lost.

I didn’t recruit from “fitness enthusiasts.” I posted in r/loseit, physical‑therapy subs, and a Discord for post‑partum return‑to‑exercise. Recorded 40+ conversations. Kept transcripts open while writing every screen.

  • 40+ user interviews
  • Competitive teardown · 12 apps
  • JTBD synthesis
02
// WK 3–5 · DEFINITION

Pick a brutal positioning, then defend it.

“If a workout makes the user feel inadequate on day one, we’ve failed.” Wrote it on the wall. Every feature I cut, I cut against that line. The first session ships at 8 minutes. No leaderboard. No before/after photos.

  • One-line product principle
  • Anti-feature list
  • Onboarding spec · 2 pages
03
// WK 5–13 · BUILD

Ship rough, then make it gentle.

Two‑week sprints, end‑to‑end. Next.js + Tailwind + Supabase. A test cohort of 18 from the original interviews got the build a week early plus a Loom from me every Friday. Six rounds of copy edits. Three rounds of removing things.

  • 6 sprints · weekly Looms
  • Closed cohort · n=18
  • 34 features cut · 12 shipped
04
// WK 14 → NOW · LIVE

Open the door, watch the floor.

Soft launch. No paid acquisition — just the original cohort sharing with friends who’d “tried apps before and bounced.” The retention curve held, and the support inbox told me what to build next. Still iterating weekly.

  • Public launch · Aug 2024
  • Weekly release cadence

// 03 — Design decisions

What other apps do vs. what LowLift does.

Every meaningful product choice in LowLift is a deliberate inversion of an industry default. Six examples.

Inversions, by design.

// LEFT: WHAT THE CATEGORY SHIPS — RIGHT: WHAT WE SHIP, AND WHY

CATEGORY DEFAULT
A “Day 1” workout that lasts 30–45 minutes and assumes you can hold a plank.
LOWLIFT
First session is 8 minutes, seated‑friendly. The bar is “press start.” Everything ramps from there.
CATEGORY DEFAULT
Hero imagery of muscular bodies, before/after shots, transformation testimonials.
LOWLIFT
No bodies on screen. Illustrative shapes, neutral copy, no scales, no progress photos. Anti‑shame is a UI commitment.
CATEGORY DEFAULT
Streaks that punish missed days with a reset to zero.
LOWLIFT
Soft streaks. Miss a day, keep the streak. Miss a week, the app says “welcome back” — not “you broke a 14‑day streak.”
CATEGORY DEFAULT
Locked content behind subscription on day 2.
LOWLIFT
First 30 days fully free. If we haven’t earned a habit in a month, we don’t deserve the card.
CATEGORY DEFAULT
Calorie counting bolted onto the workout flow.
LOWLIFT
No calorie surface, anywhere. The data correlates poorly with this user’s goal. We’d rather miss a metric than re‑traumatize.
CATEGORY DEFAULT
Push notifications optimized for re‑engagement (“Don’t break your streak!”).
LOWLIFT
One nudge per week, max. Tone: a friend checking in. We measured open rate, then stopped caring — retention held without the pressure.

// 06 — Reflection

What I’d do differently next time.

WHAT_WORKED
  • One-line product principle“If a workout makes the user feel inadequate on day one, we’ve failed.” Cut 34 features without a meeting.
  • Closed cohort before publicEighteen people who already trusted me caught problems a marketing site never would.
  • Solo end-to-endSpec, design, and code in one head meant no translation loss. Every Friday the build matched the brief.
WHAT_I’D_CHANGE
  • Built analytics lastI shipped without proper event tracking. The first month of retention came from a Supabase query I wrote at midnight. Should’ve been first.
  • Underspec’d the comeback“Welcome back” was a footnote. Half the qualitative wins came from people returning after 2+ weeks. That moment deserves its own design pass.
  • Held the price too longShould have charged earlier. Free was a crutch — paid users gave 4× the feedback.
